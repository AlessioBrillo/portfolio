## Context

Italian is a mid-resource language. It scores well in English-led benchmarks, but
the models behind those scores carry English-shaped tokenizers, English-biased
training mixes, and all the blind spots that come with a corpus nobody curated.
When I wanted to *understand* what a transformer actually costs — data hygiene,
tokenization, training dynamics — I decided to stop reading about pretrained
models and build one, from scratch, in the language I grew up with.

The plan: a small decoder-only transformer trained on a curated Italian corpus,
with a tokenizer trained on the same corpus. Small enough to train on a single
GPU in a day, honest enough that every decision in the stack was mine.

## Problem

Three knots are specific to Italian and easy to get wrong:

1. **Morphology grows the vocabulary.** Italian compounds and conjugates
   prodigiously. A fixed 32k-vocab BPE trained on English survives because
   English is morphologically poor; the same budget on Italian leaks
   comprehension on rare-but-valid forms (_tergiverseranno_, _interconnetteremmo_).
2. **Clitics and apostrophes break naive segmentation.** `l'`, `d'`, `un'`,
   `glielo` are punctuation-adjacent but semantically atomic. A byte-level
   tokenizer handles them fine; a whitespace-first one mangles the context
   window.
3. **Benchmark leakage is silent.** Public Italian corpora overlap heavily with
   any downstream test set.

## Approach

I designed the whole pipeline as three separate, testable stages:

### Stage 1 — Corpus curation

Deduplicated, decontaminated, licence-filtered: Wikipedia dumps, OSCAR-derived
web text, and a legal/regulatory corpus, normalized to Unicode NFC. Any document
within a held-out contamination threshold of common test sets was removed before
training, not after.

### Stage 2 — A tokenizer that speaks Italian

Byte-level BPE trained only on the curated corpus, vocabulary of 32,000.
Two checks gate it before a single training step:

```python
compression = len(encoded) / len(text_bytes)
assert compression < 1.0, "tokenizer must compress Italian, not leak it"

roundtrip = tokenizer.decode(tokenizer.encode(sample)) == sample
assert roundtrip, "byte-level BPE must round-trip losslessly"
```

A tokenizer that fails either check never sees a GPU.

### Stage 3 — Training with an eval that tells the truth

A small GPT-style decoder-only model — the exact configuration lives in
`config.yaml` in the repo (_fill in: layers, heads, dim, context length,
optimizer, schedule_). AdamW, cosine decay with warmup, gradient clipping,
checkpointing every N steps. Every checkpoint is evaluated on a held-out
Italian set that has *never* been seen by the optimizer.

> The eval set is not "the harder benchmark I found on the internet".
> It is the slice of the corpus I told myself, at the start, I would not
> touch. That discipline is the whole point of the exercise.

## What I built

The pipeline lives in a single repository (`transformer-italiano`): a data
prep CLI, a tokenizer trainer, the training loop, and a small eval harness.
The highlight is not the model size — it is that every stage ends with a
verifiable artifact:

- **Corpus** → `corpus.it.pq`, with the dedupe and contamination report
- **Tokenizer** → `tokenizer.json`, gated by its compression ratio on held-out text
- **Model** → `final.ckpt`, evaluated on the untouched held-out set

The meta-result is the one I keep coming back to:

1. **Data hygiene beats architecture.** The single largest quality jump came
   from deduplication and contamination removal, not from any model decision.
2. **Your own tokenizer is a forcing function.** Training BPE on Italian
   surfaces morphology questions you never ask with a borrowed tokenizer.
3. **Honest evals are a commitment device.** Deciding the held-out set *before*
   training prevents the whole class of "the eval drifted into the data" sins.

Would I do it differently? The model is small — a larger context and a longer
schedule would likely buy more than a larger width. But the point of the
project was never the leaderboard; it was to own the full stack end to end,
and it delivered exactly that.
