## Context

The Ascent is this site — the portfolio you are reading, and the artifact this
case study is about. It is a single-page, scroll-driven website whose scroll
*is* the design: the page climbs from paper-toned daylight to night, and the
light/dark shift is the journey, not a toggle. The signature is a tonal
crossfade driven by GSAP ScrollTrigger — an "ascent" that rises and falls like
a real flight profile, landing in the dark at the contact section.

The engineering premise is unusual: the repository is part of the portfolio.
If the site says I care about discipline, tests, and architecture, the commit
history, the ADR log, and the CI pipeline have to show it. Every architectural
decision is recorded as an immutable Architecture Decision Record (ADR); the
design system lives in `@theme` tokens; content is curated data separated from
presentation. Nothing is improvised, because the code is the showcase.

## Problem

A portfolio has to prove its claims, and the claims here are hard to prove:

1. **A tonal scroll engine must stay legible at every instant.** The backdrop
   crosses from paper to night and back. Text that sits on the wrong half of
   the flight fails WCAG contrast; text that flips at the wrong moment is
   worse than static. The crossfade has to be *engineered* — per family, per
   direction — not styled by eye.
2. **Motion must respect the user's body.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is not a
   courtesy here; it is a second, discrete implementation of the whole
   signature that must be rendered and asserted, not just coded.
3. **The single page has to coexist with real routes.** Case studies are
   shareable `/{domain}/{slug}` URLs (ADR-0005), each with its own document
   head, code-split MDX body, error boundary, and scroll restoration. Deep
   links must survive a static-host SPA deployment.
4. **The repo must survive its own standard.** A 80% coverage floor enforced
   in CI, typecheck, lint, format checks, and a browser harness that actually
   renders the crossfade — all of it has to stay green while the content grows.

## Approach

Three decisions carry the whole design:

### Decision 1 — The flight profile is data, not styling

The scroll journey is modelled as a flight: ground, climb, cruise, descent,
night (`src/lib/altitude.ts`, ADR-0010), and the tonal engine
(`useTonalEngine` + `TonalScene`) publishes the live backdrop tone through
React context (ADR-0011). Scene text follows that tone, and each text family
flips at its own per-direction equal-legibility line, computed by bisection
over the actual blend so the body family clears 4.5:1 at every instant of both
crossfades (ADR-0012).

### Decision 2 — The tonal engine is tested by a harness that renders it

Unit tests cover the pure logic (`src/lib/tone.ts`, `useTonalEngine`); the
Playwright suite (`npm run e2e`) is the only thing that runs GSAP
ScrollTrigger in a real browser. It asserts both crossfades — climb
paper->night and descent night->paper — hold under `prefers-reduced-motion`,
across five viewport projects including a reduced-motion emulation that
reaches `matchMedia` through the runner's `contextOptions`.

### Decision 3 — Content is data with tests, routes are lazy and contained

Sections read from tested content modules (`src/content/`) that return frozen
snapshots; the mosaic index is validated against the live page structure. Case
studies are MDX files resolved through a registry, code-split per study, with
a route-level error boundary so one broken study never crashes the app
(ADR-0005). Deep links return to the exact scroll position through the data
router's `ScrollRestoration`.

## What I built

The artefacts of the project are the parts of the repo that survive the
"who wrote this?" test:

- **12 immutable ADRs** (`docs/adr/`) — the architecture's `why` is
  recoverable, including the decisions that were reframed (ADR-0010) and the
  follow-ups they spawned (ADR-0011, ADR-0012).
- **A tonal engine with a regression net** — `useTonalEngine`, `TonalScene`,
  `tone-context`, `src/lib/tone.ts`, each with unit tests, plus the Playwright
  signature harness that catches what no unit test can.
- **A quality floor enforced, not promised** — typecheck, ESLint, Prettier,
  Vitest with an 80% coverage threshold, Husky pre-commit, commitlint
  Conventional Commits, and a CI pipeline that runs them all; the browser
  harness runs on every relevant change via path-filtered workflows.
- **Accessibility as a measured result** — a Lighthouse run on headless
  Chrome with reduced motion scored 100 on accessibility, 100 on
  best-practices, zero CLS; small-text contrast is AA-safe on every surface.
- **Self-hosted typography** — Latin-subset variable fonts (Archivo Black / Geist Sans / JetBrains Mono) committed with their OFL licences (ADR-0007): the typography
  cannot rot behind a CDN.

## Result / Reflection

The meta-result is the same one the VDS licence taught, mirrored in code:

1. **Decision hygiene beats cleverness.** The best engineering decision in
   this repo is the *discipline* one: record why, keep the files small, never
   mutate, and let the CI say no. The scroll engine is the scenographic
   exception, and it is the exception on purpose (ADR-0004, ADR-0010).
2. **An invariant you test is real.** Equal legibility through a live blend
   is not a design opinion once the harness asserts both crossfades in both
   motion modes — it is a contract the repo can keep.
3. **The repo is the resume.** The commit history, the ADR log, the coverage
   floor, and the e2e harness do the talking that a "tech stack" line in a CV
   cannot.

Building this site rewired how I approach every project:

1. **The flight profile is the architecture.** Modelling the scroll journey as ground → climb → cruise → descent → night (`src/lib/altitude.ts`) forced every section to earn its altitude. In my other work, I now start with a "flight plan" before a single line of code: what is the ground truth, where is the cruise (the serious core a reviewer sees), and how do we land? If a feature does not map to a band, it does not ship.

2. **An invariant you test is real; an invariant you document is aspirational.** Equal legibility through a live blend (ADR-0012) was a design opinion until the Playwright harness asserted both crossfades in both motion modes across five viewports. Now it is a contract the repo keeps. I carry this into every system: if the correctness property cannot be asserted in CI, it is not a requirement — it is a hope.

3. **The tonal engine is the scenographic exception, and it is the exception on purpose** (ADR-0004, ADR-0010). Everything else — typecheck, lint, format, coverage, bundle budget, photo contract, deploy routing — is disciplined, boring, and enforced. The only thing that flies is the signature. In my other work, I now explicitly designate *one* scenographic exception per project and constrain everything else to the quality floor. The contrast makes the exception visible and the floor defensible.

4. **Content-last shipping is honest.** The structure waited on the words, the photos, and the domain. If the structure is the discipline, the content is the test: whether it survives real images, real copy, and a real deployment is the next check-ride. That mindset — build the runway first, then land the aircraft — is the transferable part.

Would I do it differently? The site shipped content-last, which is honest —
the structure is now waiting on the words, the photos, and the domain. If the
structure is the discipline, the content is the test: whether it survives
real images, real copy, and a real deployment is the next check-ride.
